Appendices are lettered, and their sections, equations, figures and tables are numbered with that letter. This example appendix derives the vorticity equation from the incompressible momentum equation, $\mathrm{D}\vec{u}/\mathrm{D}t = -\rho^{-1} \grad p + \nu \nabla^2 \vec{u}$.

\section{Taking the curl of the momentum equation}\label{app:sec:curl}

Using the vector identity
\begin{equation}
    \left( \vec{u} \cdot \grad \right) \vec{u} = \grad \left( \tfrac{1}{2} \, \vec{u} \cdot \vec{u} \right) - \vec{u} \times \vec{\omega},
    \label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
with the vorticity $\vec{\omega} = \curl \vec{u}$, and taking the curl of the momentum equation, the pressure term vanishes because the curl of a gradient is zero. What remains is
\begin{equation}
    \pderiv{\vec{\omega}}{t} - \curl \left( \vec{u} \times \vec{\omega} \right) = \nu \nabla^2 \vec{\omega}.
    \label{eq:vorticity-intermediate}
\end{equation}

\section{Simplifying for incompressible flow}\label{app:sec:simplify}

For an incompressible flow, $\div \vec{u} = 0$, and $\div \vec{\omega} = 0$ always holds. Expanding the second term of \cref{eq:vorticity-intermediate} then gives the vorticity equation,
\begin{equation}
    \sderiv{\vec{\omega}}{t} = \left( \vec{\omega} \cdot \grad \right) \vec{u} + \nu \nabla^2 \vec{\omega},
    \label{eq:vorticity}
\end{equation}
in which the first term on the right-hand side describes vortex stretching and tilting, and the second the diffusion of vorticity by viscosity. \Cref{tab:appendix} summarises the terms.
