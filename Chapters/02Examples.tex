\section{Text}\label{sec:text}
%% =====================================================================

Body text is set in Times with indented paragraphs. Use \emph{emphasis} sparingly, \textbf{bold} even more so, and \textsc{small capitals} for the occasional acronym that should not shout. Quotation marks follow British style: \enquote{single quotes outside, with \enquote{double quotes} inside}. Numbers and units stay together on one line, as in a span of \qty{1.397}{\metre}, and references never start a line on their own, as in \cref{fig:single}.%
\footnote{Footnotes are numbered per chapter and set in a smaller size at the bottom of the page.}

\subsection{Lists}

An unordered list:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the first item;
    \item the second item, which is long enough to show how a list item wraps onto a second line;
    \item the third item.
\end{itemize}
An ordered list:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item pre-processing,
    \item simulation,
    \item post-processing.
\end{enumerate}
A description list:
\begin{description}
    \item[Promotor] the professor who supervises the doctorate;
    \item[Copromotor] a second supervisor, often the daily supervisor.
\end{description}

\subsection{Quotations}

Longer quotations are set apart from the text:
\begin{quote}
    \lipsum[1][1-3]
\end{quote}

\section{Cross-references and citations}\label{sec:references}
%% =====================================================================

Cross-references name the thing they point to: \cref{sec:text} discusses text, \cref{eq:navier-stokes} is the momentum equation, \cref{fig:subfigures} has two parts, of which \cref{subfig:left} is the left one, \cref{tab:geometry} lists the model geometry and \cref{app:derivation} contains a derivation. Several references at once are combined: \cref{eq:continuity,eq:navier-stokes}. \Cref{fig:plot} is on \cpageref{fig:plot}.

Citations use numbered references~\cite{Allen2002}, several at once~\cite{Sinnige2020,Bettini2021,Eitelberg2020}, or name the authors, as in \citet{Herrez2016}, who published in \citeyear{Herrez2016}. A page or table can be added to a citation~\cite[Tab.~4]{Bettini2021}.

\section{Mathematics}\label{sec:maths}
%% =====================================================================

Inline maths such as $\Re = \rho U L / \mu$ uses the same Times letters as the text. Vectors are bold and underlined, $\vec{u}$, and second-order tensors carry two bars, $\barbelow{\barbelow{\bm{\tau}}}$. For an incompressible flow, mass and momentum conservation read
\begin{align}
    \div \vec{u} &= 0, \label{eq:continuity} \\
    \sderiv{\vec{u}}{t} &= -\frac{1}{\rho} \grad p + \nu \nabla^2 \vec{u}, \label{eq:navier-stokes}
\end{align}
where the material derivative is
\begin{equation}
    \sderiv{\vec{u}}{t} = \pderiv{\vec{u}}{t} + \left( \vec{u} \cdot \grad \right) \vec{u}.
\end{equation}

A derivation over several lines numbers only its result:
\begin{align}
    \oint_{\Gamma} \vec{u} \cdot \diff{\vec{l}}
        &= \iint_{S} \left( \curl \vec{u} \right) \cdot \vec{n} \, \diff{S} \notag \\
        &= \iint_{S} \vec{\omega} \cdot \vec{n} \, \diff{S}. \label{eq:circulation}
\end{align}

Derivatives of any order, and their compact forms:
\begin{equation*}
    \deriv{y}{x}, \quad \deriv[2]{y}{x}, \quad \pderiv[2]{\phi}{x}, \quad \cpderiv{\phi}{x}, \quad \vec{A}\transpose \vec{B}, \quad \barbelow{\barbelow{\bm{\sigma}}} \cddot \barbelow{\barbelow{\bm{\varepsilon}}}.
\end{equation*}

Piecewise definitions, matrices and operators:
\begin{gather}
    C_L(\alpha) =
    \begin{cases}
        2\pi\alpha,             & |\alpha| < \alpha_\text{stall}, \\
        C_{L,\text{max}},       & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
    \qquad
    \barbelow{\barbelow{\bm{R}}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\
        \sin\theta & \cos\theta
    \end{bmatrix}, \\
    \hat{\vec{x}} = \argmin_{\vec{x}} \, \lVert \barbelow{\barbelow{\bm{A}}} \vec{x} - \vec{b} \rVert_2^2 .
\end{gather}
Dimensionless numbers are upright: $\Re$, $\Ma$, $\Pr$, $\St$ and $\Fr$.

\section{Units, numbers and chemistry}\label{sec:units}
%% =====================================================================

Quantities are written with \texttt{siunitx}, so spacing and formatting are always consistent: a density of \qty{1.225}{\kilo\gram\per\cubic\metre}, a velocity range of \qtyrange{10}{40}{\metre\per\second}, an angle of \ang{4.5}, a large number such as \num{6.02214076e23} or \num{1234567.891}, and a product of dimensions \qtyproduct{1.342 x 0.140}{\metre}. Units on their own are written as \unit{\newton\metre\per\second}. Chemical formulae and reactions use \texttt{mhchem}: \ce{CO2}, \ce{H2O}, and \ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}.

\section{Code}\label{sec:code}
%% =====================================================================

Short pieces of code appear inline, such as \lstinline{np.linspace(0, 1, 11)}, and longer ones as listings (\cref{lst:example}).

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Computing the lift coefficient from thin-aerofoil theory.}, label={lst:example}]
import numpy as np

def lift_coefficient(alpha_deg):
    # Thin-aerofoil theory: C_L = 2 pi alpha
    alpha = np.radians(alpha_deg)
    return 2 * np.pi * alpha

for alpha in range(0, 12, 2):
    print(f"alpha = {alpha:2d} deg, C_L = {lift_coefficient(alpha):.3f}")
\end{lstlisting}
